The founder of Studio Illusions

Dalvy Joseph.

Before the pastry garden, there was a photography studio.

Dalvy Joseph started Studio Illusions in the 1980s. The studio’s own history traces its path from photography to work with the BBC and Emirates, and later to Forwardable.

His compound is the setting for Pâte à Chaya. The idea starts with what is already here — the cottage, the bamboo and the room to gather.

Franchise readiness

prove the second unit.

The flagship pays to document the recipes, train operators and audit quality. A second location still needs a viable lease and enough sales to carry a royalty.

Prove paid demand.

Run prepaid pickup sessions. Count paid boxes, repeat buyers, refunds and waste. Compare the actual contribution with the pilot’s ₹240 per direct box.

Repeat the bake.

Have a second baker reproduce three batches from the same approved recipe. Audit saleable yield, finished weight, cold-chain records and traceability.

Underwrite a lease.

Run the calculator with a cash rent and a royalty. Compare results with the owned flagship before offering territory or promising a payback date.
